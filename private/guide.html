Chapter 00

Start here.

In 2021 I weighed 125 pounds. I was skinny, I had little to no muscle, and I had abs only because there was nothing covering them. In 2026 I weigh 185. I did it with no supplements, no steroids, no peptides, and no caffeine. This is everything I learned on the way.

I'm not a coach and this isn't a textbook. It's the conversation I'd have with you if you asked me at the gym how I did it, written down so you can come back to it. Some of this is science. Some of it is six years of trial and error. I'll tell you which is which.

The three things that actually changed my body

  1. Nutrition. The number one thing. I started prioritizing red meat: steak, ground beef, all of it. I eat a lot of it and I get my protein and creatine from the plate. Everything else in this guide is secondary to this.
  2. Lower reps, heavier weight. I spent months doing sets of 12, 15, 20. When I dropped to 3–8 reps and started going to failure I found out I was a lot stronger than I thought. Muscle followed strength.
  3. Removing the thing that was draining me. My biggest roadblock was porn. It was taking my attention, my energy and my self-respect. Cutting it out is what made consistency possible. Chapter 06 is honest about it.

Two more things about the transformation

  • My core lifts went up the most. Bench, squat, deadlift. That's because my football coach trained me like a powerlifter and made me perfect my form before he let me load the bar. Chapter 02 is built on that.
  • The hardest muscle to grow was my calves. I came from skinny and I still lack calves because of injuries. They're finally getting stronger now that I train them consistently. Everyone has a stubborn muscle. Train it anyway.
If I could tell my old self one thing: stay consistent. You learn from actions, not words. Failing and embarrassing yourself is what creates the successful version of you.

How to use this guide

Read Chapter 01 and 02 and pick a split. Read Chapter 03, run the calculator, and set your protein and fat. Then go train. Don't try to do all eleven chapters at once. The guide will be here. The hard part is Monday, and then the Monday after that.

Before you start

This is my experience and general information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, an injury, or you're under 18, talk to a doctor before changing how you train or eat.

Chapter 01

Training splits, science-backed.

A split is just how you divide your muscles across the week. The research is pretty clear on the part that matters: hitting a muscle twice a week beats once a week for growth, as long as total work is similar. So every split below hits everything at least twice.

I trained each muscle once a week for a long time. Chest Monday, back Tuesday, the classic bro split. I got somewhere, then I stopped getting anywhere for a couple of years. Moving to twice a week per muscle changed my size and strength more than anything else I did in the gym.

How to pick one

Pick by how many days you can actually train, not how many you wish you could. A four-day split you never miss beats a six-day split you do half of.

Chapter 02

How to run a session.

The split tells you what day it is. This chapter is what you do when you walk in. Same warm-up, heaviest lift first, 3–8 reps on the things that matter, then volume.

Warm up the same way every time

Since high school football I've warmed up the same way on leg and athletic days, and I'm not changing it. Routine is the point. When the warm-up is automatic, you never skip it, and you walk up to the bar already switched on.

  • 5 minutes of movement. Walk, bike, or jump rope. You're raising body temperature, not getting a workout.
  • Dynamic work. Leg swings, hip openers, walking lunges, high knees, arm circles. Two or three rounds, nothing static.
  • Ramp-up sets. On your first lift: empty bar, then 50%, 70%, 85% of your working weight for a few reps each. Then the real sets.

The core lifts: train like a powerlifter

I train like a powerlifter to gain the most strength while staying lean. On the core lifts, bench, squat, deadlift and power clean, I do 3 sets of 3 pause reps at 85% of my max. A pause rep means you stop dead at the bottom (bar on the chest, bottom of the squat, bar on the floor) for a full second, then drive. No bounce, no stretch reflex, nothing to hide behind. It forces perfect form and builds strength faster than anything else I've done.

These are the lifts that built me, and they're the ones you should get strong on before you worry about anything else. Every session is built around one of them.

LiftBuildsMy notes
SquatQuads, glutes, core, everything3 × 3 pause reps at 85%. Depth over weight. If it's not at least parallel it doesn't count.
Bench pressChest, shoulders, triceps3 × 3 pause reps at 85%. Feet planted, shoulder blades back, pause on the chest.
DeadliftBack, hamstrings, glutes, grip3 × 3 at 85%, dead stop on the floor every rep. Once a week.
Power cleanWhole body, explosiveness3 × 3 at 85%. Football carryover. Skip it until someone has taught you the form.

My exercises, muscle by muscle

You only need to hit each muscle group for 2–3 hard sets per exercise if you actually train hard enough. Not ten sets. Two or three that you couldn't have done another rep of. After the core lift, these are the movements I build the rest of the session from.

The rep range nobody in the gym will tell you

Ten-plus reps is too much for the lifts that matter. If you want muscle and strength, your main work is 3–8 reps. I spent months in the 12–20 range feeling a pump and going nowhere. When I lowered the reps and raised the weight, my body changed.

Core lift

3 × 3 pause reps

Your heaviest lift of the day, first, at 85% of your max. This is where strength comes from.

Secondary lifts

5–8 reps

The next one or two compound moves. 2–3 hard sets each. Still heavy, slightly more reps.

Isolation

6–8 reps

Curls, pulldowns, extensions, calves. 2–3 hard sets. This is the "volume" part of the session.

Going to failure

Last set only

Take the final set of your main and secondary lifts to failure, or one rep shy. Leave the rest in the tank.

Heaviest first, volume after, same order every time

Start every session with the heaviest, most demanding lift while you're fresh. Then the secondary lifts, then the isolation work. By the end you're doing volume with lighter loads and the hard part is already done. Doing it the other way around, tiring yourself out on curls and then trying to squat, is how you get hurt and go backwards.

And keep the order fixed. If you switch the order of your exercises week to week, your strength on each one will fluctuate and you won't know whether you're actually progressing. Same lifts, same order, and the numbers tell the truth.

Form over weight. Always.

Without proper form you can't actually target the muscle, and you will hurt yourself. I'm not saying lift light forever. I'm saying perfect the movement first, then add weight progressively. Film your sets. If the last rep looks nothing like the first, the weight is too heavy for today.

You are stronger than you think

I was doing high weight for high reps and I thought that was my ceiling. When I increased the weight and pushed a set to actual failure, I realized I had way more in me. I'd been limiting myself for months. Once a week, on your main lift, find out where failure really is. It's usually further than you think.

Progression, kept simple

  1. Core lift: once all three pause triples at 85% feel solid for two sessions in a row, add 5 lb (upper) or 10 lb (lower). Re-test your max every 8–12 weeks and recalculate 85%.
  2. Everything else: pick a weight you can do for the bottom of the rep range with clean form. Each week add a rep per set until you hit the top. Then add weight and go back to the bottom.
  3. Stalled for three weeks? Drop the weight 10% and climb again. That's normal.
A session, start to finish

Warm-up (10 min) → Core lift, 3 × 3 pause reps at 85% → Secondary lift, 2–3 × 5–8 → Secondary lift, 2–3 × 5–8 → Two or three isolation moves, 2–3 × 6–8 → Walk out. Sixty to seventy-five minutes.

Diet structures.

Nutrition was the number one thing I changed and it's the number one thing I'd tell you to change. Here's how I set up protein, fat and carbs, how I bulk without losing my abs, how I cut, and how carb cycling fits in.

Calories are a starting point (bodyweight × 15, then ± for the phase). Weigh yourself every morning, average the week, and adjust by 100–200 calories if the scale isn't moving the way you want.

Lean bulk: adding size without losing your abs

This is where I live most of the year. The goal is to gain slowly enough that most of it is muscle.

  • Protein at 1 g per lb. Fat at the higher end, 0.4–0.5 g per lb. Red meat does both of these for you.
  • Calories at maintenance plus 200–300. Not plus 1,000. A "dirty bulk" just makes you fat and then you spend four months cutting it off.
  • Scale target: about half a pound a week. If you're gaining faster than a pound a week, pull carbs back a little.
  • Keep your steps up. Walking is how you stay lean on a bulk.

Cutting: getting lean while keeping what you built

  • Protein stays at 1 g per lb. This is non-negotiable when you're in a deficit.
  • Fat drops to the low end, 0.3 g per lb. Pick leaner cuts, trim the fat off the steak.
  • Calories at maintenance minus 300–500. Lose about a pound a week. Faster than that and you're losing muscle.
  • Carbs go around training. Keep the pre-workout carbs. Cut the rest from the days you don't train.
  • Keep lifting heavy. The weights are the signal that tells your body to keep the muscle.

Maintain / recomp

If you're new, or coming back after time off, you can build muscle and lose fat at the same time at maintenance calories with protein high. Eat at maintenance, lift hard, walk a lot, and let the mirror be the judge instead of the scale.

Carb cycling, without the spreadsheet

Carb cycling just means eating more carbs on the days you train and fewer on the days you don't. Your body uses carbs for the work you're asking of it, so give it more when the work is hard.

Training days

High carb

Pre-workout carbs plus rice, potatoes, sourdough through the day. Fat a little lower.

Rest days

Low carb

Protein and fat stay. Carbs come down by roughly a third to a half. Eggs, meat, vegetables.

Over the week your average is the same as the calculator says. You just move the carbs to where they do work. On a cut this is how I keep training hard while losing fat. On a bulk it keeps me from gaining fat on the days I'm sitting around.

Fast carbs before you train

I don't take pre-workout and I don't drink caffeine. My pre-workout is 50–100 g of fast-digesting carbs 30 to 60 minutes before I lift. White rice, a potato, sourdough with honey, fruit, even a sports drink. You walk into the gym full of fuel and you don't crash at the end.

Whole foods, or don't bother

Nutrition is the key to body fat. If you're eating junk, you will not progress like someone eating clean, no matter how hard you train. Eat whole foods, cut out processed food, and you'll see the best progression of your life. Seed oils and processed food fog your mind, and in my experience they're why people burn in the sun so easily. The easiest rule I know: if it isn't in Minecraft, don't eat it.

Hydration and electrolytes

You sweat while you sleep. Start the day with water, before coffee and before food. Then keep drinking through the whole day, with electrolytes: a pinch of sea salt in your water does it. If you aren't drinking water and electrolytes all day, you will not have high energy, full stop. A good target is around half your bodyweight in ounces, more if you're training or it's hot. Being even slightly dehydrated makes every set feel heavier than it is.

The short version

Protein 1 g per lb. Fat 0.3–0.5 g per lb depending on the phase. Fast carbs before you train, more carbs on training days, fewer on rest days. Red meat does most of the heavy lifting. Adjust by the scale weekly, not daily.

Chapter 05

Supplements: what helps, what doesn't.

Six years in, I take nothing. No protein powder, no creatine, no pre-workout, no caffeine. Steroid and peptide free. I'm not telling you that to be preachy. I'm telling you so you know the body on the front of this site was built with food.

Why I don't take any

  • Protein comes from red meat and eggs. I hit 1 g per lb without trying.
  • Creatine comes from red meat. A pound of beef has a few grams in it. Eat enough and you're covered.
  • Pre-workout is 50–100 g of fast carbs 30–60 minutes before I train. I've never had a better one.
  • Caffeine I skip entirely. My energy comes from sleep, food and water, and it doesn't crash at 3 pm.

If you want to take something, this is the list

Three things. That's it.

Worth it

Grass-fed protein powder

Only if you can't hit your protein from food. The regular big-name brands test high for metals and aren't clean, and they'll wreck your gut. If you use a powder, make it grass-fed.

Worth it

Black coffee with raw butter

If you want caffeine, this is how I'd do it. Very clean, no ultra-processed syrups and sugar like a Starbucks order. One cup, in the morning.

Worth it

Clean creatine powder

One to two scoops a day, and drink a lot of water with it. I get mine naturally from red meat, but if you're not eating a pound of beef a day, this is the one supplement with real evidence behind it.

Not on the list

Everything else

Pre-workouts, fat burners, mass gainers, BCAAs, test boosters. See below.

What I'd skip

  • Pre-workout powders. Mostly caffeine and tingles. Eat a banana with honey instead.
  • Fat burners. They don't. A deficit and steps do.
  • Mass gainers. Sugar in a tub. Eat a loaded potato.
  • BCAAs. If you're eating enough protein these do nothing.
  • Test boosters. Sleep, red meat, walking and not watching porn did more for me than any pill could.

On steroids and peptides

A lot of the people you compare yourself to are on something. That's not a judgment, it's just true, and it's why comparing yourself to them is pointless. There's no need for any of it. Progress comes with time. I'm proof you can go from 125 to 185 without touching it.

One line

Food first. If you need a supplement to hit a number, use the simplest one that does it. Nothing else.

Chapter 06

Mindset progression.

Everything in this guide works if you show up. The problem is never the plan. It's the fourth week when nothing's changed yet, or the night you'd rather do anything else. This chapter is about how I built the mental framework that keeps me consistent.

Stop comparing yourself to other people

The guy next to you has been training for eight years. The guy on your feed is on gear. Most of the people you measure yourself against have either been doing this for a long time or are taking peptides or steroids, and you're comparing your month three to their year eight. There is no need for any of that. Progress comes with time. Compare yourself to the photo you took last month, not to anyone else.

You're not behind. You just started.

Find a routine and keep it

Decide once, then stop deciding. Same days, same time, same warm-up, same exercise order. I've warmed up the same way since high school football. The gym is not where you want to be making decisions; it's where you execute the ones you already made. When training is a fixed part of your day like brushing your teeth, motivation stops mattering.

You learn from actions, not words

If I could tell my old self one thing it would be: stay consistent. Nobody gets this from reading, including from reading this guide. You get it from doing it, failing at it, embarrassing yourself under a bar that was too heavy, and coming back the next day. Achieving failure and embarrassing yourself is exactly what creates the successful version of you.

The habit I had to kill

My biggest roadblock was porn. I used to watch it multiple times a day. One day it clicked: I was giving my energy to pixels on a screen, to women who didn't know I existed, and getting nothing back. I was looking at girls only in a sexual way and chasing them. I felt it pulling me somewhere dark. I'm being honest about this because nobody told me, and I think a lot of guys reading this are in the same spot.

When I cut it out, I got my attention back. I got my drive back. I started prioritizing myself and my health, and that's when the consistency showed up. If this is you, here's what worked for me:

  • Make it inconvenient. Phone out of the bedroom. Blockers on. No private browsing.
  • Replace it, don't just remove it. The urge is a signal that you're bored, stressed or tired. Walk. Train. Cook. Call someone.
  • Count days. A streak is motivating. Breaking one isn't a reason to quit, it's a reason to start day one again.
  • Redirect the energy. The discipline it takes to stop is the same discipline that gets you to the gym. Use it.

The framework for maximum aesthetic progress

  1. Pick the goal for the next 12 weeks. Bulk or cut. Not both. Write it down.
  2. Make the plan boring. One split. The same meals. The same bedtime. Boring is what you can repeat.
  3. Track three things. Weight on the bar, weight on the scale, a photo every two weeks. Nothing else.
  4. Don't miss. Period. Never skipping a day in the gym made my day. You should be pissed if you miss the gym. No matter what, don't miss and backtrack days. Over time this builds a mental adaptation: showing up stops being a decision.
  5. Judge every 12 weeks, not every day. The mirror lies day to day. It doesn't lie over three months.

When you want to quit

You will. Usually around week four and week ten. That's when the newness is gone and the results haven't fully shown up yet. Know that it's coming, and that it's the exact point where most people stop and go back to 125 pounds. Go to the gym anyway. Do the warm-up. The rest follows.

What I tell myself

Nobody is coming to do this for you. That's not a bad thing. It means it's yours.

Chapter 07

Cardio, steps & recovery.

I'm going to say something you won't hear from most trainers: for building a lean, muscular body, jogging is a waste of your time. Walk, or sprint.

Why I stopped jogging

Long, slow jogging burns a few calories and leaves you drained for the thing that actually changes your body, which is lifting. In my experience, months of steady jogging worked against me: I felt flat, my lifts stalled, and I looked no better for it. I believe it was dragging my hormones and recovery down. I'm not a doctor, but the day I stopped jogging and started walking and sprinting, everything got better.

Walk 10,000 to 30,000 steps a day

This is the cardio. It burns fat without eating into recovery, it keeps you lean on a bulk, it clears your head, and it's free. When I bumped my steps up I felt like my energy and drive went through the roof. Ten thousand is the floor. On a cut I push toward twenty or thirty.

  • Walk first thing in the morning after your water. Sun on your face.
  • Walk after meals. Ten minutes after dinner does more than you'd think.
  • Take calls walking. Park far. Take the stairs. It adds up fast.

Sprint once or twice a week

Sprinting builds muscle, especially in your legs and glutes, and it's over in fifteen minutes. On a rest day or after a light session:

  1. Warm up with your normal dynamic routine.
  2. 6–10 sprints of 60–100 meters, at 80–90% effort. Not all-out on the first one.
  3. Walk back fully between each. Rest is the point.
  4. Walk home.

Rest is the most important factor, with hydration

If you're running on no sleep, there's no room for your muscles to recover. None. Missing sleep also lowers your testosterone as a man. Every bit of muscle you build is built while you're asleep, so if you're cutting sleep to fit in more training you have it backwards.

Quality over quantity. You can lie in bed for eight hours, but eight hours of low-quality sleep is worse than five hours of deep sleep. Same bedtime every night. Phone out of the room, which also helps with Chapter 06. Last meal three hours before bed, no chugging water right before, cotton sheets. Chapter 08 goes deeper on the setup.

Rest days

Rest days are walking days. You still eat your protein. You still drink your water. You keep carbs lower. You don't sit on the couch all day feeling guilty, and you don't sneak in a seventh gym session. Recovery is where the growth happens.

Chapter 08

Beyond the gym: testosterone & health.

What will really boost your testosterone is simple: working out, 10,000-plus steps a day, eating whole foods, and cotton boxers. Now that we've covered the training and the food, here's everything else I changed around the house that made me feel, sleep and recover better.

None of this replaces the gym and the plate. It's the layer on top. I didn't do all of it at once. I swapped one thing at a time whenever something wore out, and over a couple of years my whole house changed.

Clothing

If you're wearing polyester boxers, you're heating your balls up in plastic all day. Switch to 100% cotton boxers first, then cotton for as much of the rest of your clothing as you can. Organic if you can afford it, plain cotton if you can't.

Blue light

Most of us are on a screen or playing games at night. Red-lens blue light glasses in the evening cut the exposure a lot. Wear them from sunset until bed. I keep a pair next to the couch so there's no excuse.

Sleep

  • Last meal three hours before bed. Your body should be sleeping, not digesting.
  • Don't chug water before sleeping. You'll wake up in the middle of the night to use the bathroom and break your deepest sleep.
  • 100% cotton sheets, blanket and clothing. Out of everything in this chapter, I think this moved my sleep and my testosterone the most.
  • Sunrise alarm. Wake up to light instead of a sound. You get up calmer and it sets your rhythm for the day.

Chapter 09

What I wish I knew.

If I could hand my 125-pound self one page, it would be this one.

  1. Do not compare yourself to other people.

    Most of them have been in the gym or the fitness industry for years. A lot of them take peptides or steroids. There's no need for that. Progress comes with time.

  2. Form over weight.

    Without proper form you can't directly target the muscle and you'll hurt yourself. Perfect your form as you progressively increase the weight. Not before, not after. As.

  3. Train every muscle at least twice a week.

    I trained each muscle once a week and after a couple of years I stopped seeing any progress at all. Twice a week increased my size and strength by a lot. This is the biggest training change I made.

  4. Don't miss a day.

    Never skipping the gym made my day. You should be pissed if you miss. No matter what, don't miss and backtrack. It builds a mental adaptation as much as a physical one.

  5. Find a routine and keep it.

    Same warm-up since high school football. Same exercise order, because switching the order makes your strength fluctuate. Heaviest workouts first, then the volume work.

  6. Rest is the most important factor, with hydration.

    No sleep, no recovery, and lower testosterone. Quality beats quantity: five hours of deep sleep beats eight bad ones.

  7. Start your day hydrating.

    Your body sweats while you sleep. Water first, then water and electrolytes all day. Without them you will not have high energy.

  8. Nutrition is the key to body fat.

    Eat junk and you won't progress like someone eating clean. Whole foods in, processed foods out. If it isn't in Minecraft, don't eat it.

  9. 10+ reps is too much.

    Nobody in the gym will tell you this. If you want to gain muscle and strength, do 3–8 reps on the lifts that matter.

  10. Train the core lifts like a powerlifter.

    Bench, squat, deadlift, power clean: 3 sets of 3 pause reps at 85% of your max. The most strength while staying lean. Everything else is a detail.

  11. Jogging is a waste. Walk or sprint.

    Walk 10–30k steps a day. Sprint once or twice a week. Your energy, your physique and in my experience your drive all go up.

  12. You are stronger than you think.

    I was doing high weight for high reps. When I increased the weight and went to failure I found out I'd been limiting myself. Find your real limit, on purpose, once a week.

  13. Red meat first.

    Prioritize grass-fed red meat. Eat enough protein and fat. I'd trade every supplement on earth for this.

  14. Carbs are literal energy.

    50–100 g of fast carbs 30–60 minutes before you train, heavier carbs through the rest of the day, fewer on rest days. That's carb cycling.

  15. Kill the thing that's draining you.

    For me it was porn. For you it might be something else. Whatever is quietly taking your energy every day is costing you more than any workout can give back.

Chapter 10

Your first 12 weeks.

Here's how I'd put it all together if you were starting Monday. Keep it exactly this simple.

Before Monday

Set it up once

Pick your split by the days you can really train. Run the calculator. Buy the groceries from Chapter 04. Put the phone outside the bedroom. Take a front and side photo.

Weeks 1–4

Learn the lifts, build the routine

Weights are moderate. The goal is to not miss a session, nail your warm-up, and get your form right on the core lifts. Hit protein every day. Ten thousand steps. Don't judge anything yet.

Weeks 5–8

Go heavy

Core lifts move to 3 × 3 pause reps at 85%. Everything else 2–3 hard sets, last set to failure. Add weight when you hit the top of the range. This is where you find out you're stronger than you think. Photo at week 6.

Weeks 9–12

Dial the diet

Now that training is automatic, tighten the nutrition. Carb cycle. Adjust calories by the scale. Add one sprint session a week. Week 12: photos, compare to week 0, decide the next 12 weeks.

What to expect

In 12 weeks, if you don't miss, you'll be noticeably stronger on every core lift and you'll see it in the photos. You won't look like a different person yet. I didn't at 12 weeks either. It took me five years to go from 125 to 185, and the first 12 weeks were just the proof that it was working. That's all you need. Proof, then repeat.

Progress comes with time.
